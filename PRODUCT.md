# Hamza Anjum — CV

## Product Purpose

A personal CV/resume presented as a static HTML page. The document is the product. It must read and print beautifully, convey technical seniority at a glance, and feel native to the tools and ecosystems its author works in daily: VS Code, GitHub, Azure, the Microsoft Power Platform.

## Register

brand — this is a portfolio/CV; design IS the product.

## Users

Hiring managers and technical leads at Microsoft partner consultancies, UK government digital teams, and enterprise software firms. They skim, then read. They open on a laptop, sometimes print to PDF.

## Tone

Confident. Senior. Precise. No fluff. The copy should read like well-commented code: only what earns its place.

## Brand

- **Name:** Hamza Anjum
- **Role:** Senior Dynamics 365 / Power Platform Developer
- **Location:** Manchester, UK

## Anti-references

- Generic FlowCV or Resume.io templates — avoid the default two-column with teal sidebar
- "Creative agency" portfolios with elaborate animations or gradient blobs
- The SaaS-blue hero-metric layout (big number, small label, gradient accent)
- Glassmorphism cards
- Any design that looks obviously AI-generated

## Strategic principles

1. Monospace font everywhere — JetBrains Mono. Non-negotiable. This is the core aesthetic signal.
2. Dark IDE theme — the CV should feel like it was made in the same tool the developer uses every day.
3. Every design element should justify itself. No decoration for decoration's sake.
4. Print-ready from day one. A4 format, colors must survive `print-color-adjust: exact`.
5. Company logos as 24px circles next to each employer — visual credibility, not clutter.
